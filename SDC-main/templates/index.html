How your match is calculated

An explainable information-retrieval baseline combines your skills, interests and public job signals. No language model is used to generate careers or courses.

65% Skills coverage

How much of the role's weighted skill profile you already have.

20% Interest relevance

Lexical similarity between your stated interests and the role description, using TF–IDF.

15% Market signal

Observed role-title and skill mentions in the current sample. Scenarios change skill priorities, not posting counts.

A score is an exploration aid, not a hiring probability. A lexical baseline can miss transferable skills and synonyms.

Fresh signals, honest limitations

Public job posts are mapped to a curated skills and roles catalogue. New evidence can change match scores and rankings. AI release signals can adjust assumed skill priorities.

  • Source URLs, posting dates and refresh time stay visible.
  • Recent and earlier windows measure the direction of this sample. Source refreshes are limited to once every five minutes.
  • Limited evidence is shown explicitly instead of inventing a trend.
  • If the source is unavailable, the last cached snapshot is labelled.
  • International graduate vacancies are not Malaysian employment statistics.
Moderate and fast AI adoption use illustrative assumptions. They do not estimate how many jobs AI will create or replace.

People stay in charge

NextStep supports career exploration. HR makes application decisions manually in a synthetic recruitment workspace.

  • Only public postings and synthetic demo profiles.
  • No age, gender, ethnicity, or other protected attributes in matching.
  • Excluding protected attributes does not prove fairness; evaluation is still needed.
  • Guests can explore without an account. Signed-in resume and company forms are stored locally in the demo database; use synthetic details.
  • Learning progress stays in your browser and can be cleared.
